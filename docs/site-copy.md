## Cutting-room floor — things to leave out

- **"I can work in any industry."** Reads as "I haven't worked in yours." Your range
  shows through two different domains in the case studies; you don't need to assert it.
- **"I can deliver any technological solution."** Same problem, louder.
- **A skills grid of twenty logos.** Nobody has ever been hired off one. The stack
  lines inside each case study do the job with context attached.
- **A contact form.** Plain email converts better and signals less friction.
- **Placeholder testimonials or fake client logos.** Obvious, and fatal.

## When you rotate this to client-facing

Later, once you have a named client and one real outcome number, the changes are
small: swap the hero's "available for roles" for a service line, add testimonials
and client logos above the fold, and rewrite the case study openers from
*decisions* to *outcomes*. The structure survives. Don't build for that day yet.
